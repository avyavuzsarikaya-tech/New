When a bridge collapses, we ask who designed it, who built it and who inspected it. We do not blame the steel. Yet when an automated system refuses a loan, flags a traveller or ranks a job application, it is tempting to say that "the algorithm decided", as though no one were responsible.

In 2021 UNESCO's member states adopted a recommendation on the ethics of artificial intelligence. Among its principles is that responsibility for decisions made with AI systems should always be attributable to people or institutions, never to the machine alone.[^1]

This matters because the people most affected by automated decisions are often the least able to challenge them. If a mistake is made, someone must be able to explain it, correct it and, where necessary, make amends.

> A tool can be powerful without being responsible. Only people can be responsible.

The Universal Declaration promises everyone an effective remedy for acts that violate their rights.[^2] A remedy is impossible without an answer to a simple question: who did this? Clever engineering cannot be allowed to make that question unanswerable.

The goal is not to fear our tools, but to keep them in their proper place: as servants of human judgement, never as a hiding place from it.
